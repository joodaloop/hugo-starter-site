Advance Market Commitments (AMCs) are contractual agreements where funders commit to purchasing a specified quantity of a product—typically one that doesn't yet exist—at a predetermined price. This mechanism reduces market uncertainty for developers and manufacturers, enabling them to invest confidently in research, development, and production capacity for products that might otherwise be considered too risky.

The most celebrated example of an AMC is the pneumococcal vaccine initiative, which committed $1.5 billion to purchase vaccines for developing countries. This commitment incentivized pharmaceutical companies to develop and manufacture vaccines they might otherwise have deprioritized due to uncertain demand in low-income markets. The result was faster vaccine development and millions of lives saved.

AMCs work best when funders can clearly specify the characteristics a product must have to qualify for purchase, when the product addresses a well-defined need, and when market uncertainty—rather than technical feasibility—is the primary barrier to innovation. They are particularly powerful for pulling innovation toward neglected markets where commercial incentives alone are insufficient.
